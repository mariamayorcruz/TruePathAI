# TruePath Intelligence Framework

**Purpose.** This document defines the intellectual foundation for a future TruePath AI assessment system. It expresses methodology, developmental logic, ethics, and product intelligence—not technical implementation.

**Audience.** Founders, product, learning science collaborators, counselors, guardians, future assessors, and engineering teams who translate principles into UX and infrastructure.

---

## 1. Core philosophy

TruePath rests on several commitments:

**Development over labeling.** Assessments illuminate trajectories—how strengths combine, where friction appears, what environments accelerate growth—not fixed identity types used to constrain people.

**Context before conclusion.** Signals from young people gain meaning alongside age, schooling context, autonomy level, linguistic background, wellbeing, and support systems.

**Potential as permission, not prophecy.** Outputs should widen realistic options and next steps rather than narrowing life paths or implying inevitability.

**Human-centered interpretation.** Any automated synthesis supports human judgment: educators, guardians, counselors, or the learner. The framework treats machine assistance as scaffolding, not replacement for care and duty of care.

**Transparency and restraint.** Methods, limitations, and confidence levels should be communicated plainly. Where uncertainty is material, say so explicitly.

Together, these principles position TruePath as a developmental intelligence layer—ethical, exploratory, and aligned with longitudinal wellbeing.

## 2. Age-based framework

The system organizes expectations and norms around **approximate developmental windows**, not birthdays alone. Calibration always allows for individual variance and cultural pacing.

### 10–12 Discovery Phase

- **Psychological stance:** Exploration, imaginative play-to-purpose, emergence of comparative self-awareness, strengthening of basic self-concept outside the family nucleus.
- **Assessment emphasis:** Breadth—interests, preferred modalities, rudimentary reasoning patterns, social comfort in structured vs. unstructured settings, early signs of perseverance.
- **Output posture:** Celebrate diversity of curiosity; avoid career specificity; prioritize language that reinforces growth mindset and optional pathways.

### 13–15 Potential Awareness Phase

- **Psychological stance:** Identity experimentation, heightened peer sensitivity, increased capacity for abstract thinking, widening time horizon (“what I might become”).
- **Assessment emphasis:** Mapping emerging strengths across cognitive, social-emotional, and motivational dimensions; spotting mismatch between aptitude and declared interests; scaffolding early self-agency without premature specialization.
- **Output posture:** Connect patterns to domains and learning environments (clubs, coursework types, mentorship) rather than to single occupations; reinforce agency and reversible choices.

### 16–18 Future Direction Phase

- **Psychological stance:** Consolidation moves toward coherence; planning tasks become salient (courses, extracurricular arcs, preliminary post-secondary framing); autonomy and stakes increase materially.
- **Assessment emphasis:** Deeper motivational drivers, endurance under ambiguity, interpersonal influence and collaboration style, realism of planning vs. exploratory dreaming, readiness for progressively adult tradeoffs.
- **Output posture:** Richer bridging to ecosystems (education pathways, exploratory career clusters, development plans) while preserving optionality and explicit uncertainty where data are thin.

**Cross-cutting rule.** Age brackets inform **priorities** and **language**, not entitlement to restrict content or withhold developmental insight framed appropriately.

## 3. Human development dimensions

The following dimensions are **observable-in-principle**: they can inform item design, evidence models, narrative synthesis, and longitudinal tracking. Each should be grounded in contextual items and corroborating signals—not single-score absolutes.

| Dimension | Working definition |
|-----------|---------------------|
| **Curiosity** | Drive to inquire, diversify experience, sustain questions in the face of incompleteness. |
| **Creativity** | Fluency of novel-but-useful ideas within constraints; comfort with iterative revision. |
| **Analytical thinking** | Structured reasoning, pattern spotting, causal hypothesizing, and evidence evaluation under age-appropriate task demands. |
| **Empathy** | Recognition of others’ feelings and perspectives; proportionate responsiveness in social vignettes or reflective prompts. |
| **Leadership** | Influence without coercion; initiative in group fairness; stewardship of collective goals versus self-promotion alone. |
| **Collaboration** | Shared problem-solving posture: listening, reciprocity, role flexibility, constructive conflict norms. |
| **Communication** | Clarity for audience and purpose across modalities relevant to MVP (written, conversational, explanatory). |
| **Resilience** | Recovery narratives and strategies after setbacks; steadiness vs. paralysis under mild stress vignettes (without clinical claims). |
| **Adaptability** | Flexibility when rules, goals, or information change constructively—not mere compliance. |
| **Autonomy** | Ownership of choices, pacing, goal-setting—with appropriate scaffolding by age—and resistance to undue external sway. |
| **Learning style** | Preferences and effective strategies across modalities (**not** labeling as innate “styles” divorced from evidence; treat as hypotheses for matching supports). |
| **Emotional awareness** | Labeling affects, distinguishing intensity from catastrophe, linkage between emotions and situational appraisals—in non-clinical framings appropriate to youth. |
| **Systems thinking** | Seeing interdependencies among people, incentives, ecosystems, timelines, unintended effects; distinguishing symptoms from upstream causes where appropriate. |
| **Risk tolerance** | Willingness to experiment under ambiguity vs. preference for certainty—without moralizing either pole. |
| **Innovation tendency** | Desire to originate or improve workflows, prototypes, narratives, or norms rather than replicate only—distinct from reckless rule-breaking. |
| **Practical execution** | Planfulness, sequencing, reliability of follow-through, time and resource realism. |
| **Social energy** | Sustainable preference for solitude, pairing, larger groups—not introversion stereotypes as deficits. |
| **Reflective depth** | Habit of metacognition: learning from mistakes, refining strategies, interrogating assumptions at age-appropriate depth. |

**Design note.** Where clinical constructs might seem adjacent (e.g., anxiety, ADHD), maintain clear **non-diagnostic boundaries** aligned with Ethics and AI Boundaries sections.

## 4. Assessment methodology

Future TruePath assessments should aspire to methodological rigor while remaining humane and participatory.

### Evidence layering

Design item families that generate **multiple weak signals per dimension**, triangulating self-report, constrained scenarios (vignettes), preference tasks, structured reflections, behavioral traces from interactive prototypes (later generations), and—where appropriate—validated short scales under license and supervision—not all at MVP.

### Longitudinal coherence

Assume **patterns mature**; revisit dimensions with anchors that preserve comparability yet respect developmental shift in meaning (vertical scaling vs. blunt reuse).

### Reducing biases

Mitigate socioeconomic, linguistic, and cultural bias via diverse item pilots, readability controls, multilingual strategy, avoidance of jargon tied to privileged subcultures, and sensitivity reviews.

### Calibration and fairness

Define fairness targets collaboratively (outcome parity where legitimate, minimized disparate impact across protected classes, differentiated treatment only when ethically justified—e.g., accessibility accommodations—not stereotype amplification).

### Reliability hygiene

Establish internal consistency thresholds where additive; prefer **measurement-informed narrative** thresholds that guard against overstating granularity.

### Transparency

Publish an accessible explanation of constructs, approximate item types, aggregation logic at a conceptual level (not concealed weights), validation status, known limitations.

### Human verification loops

Experts review edge cases—especially outliers, inconsistencies, safeguarding triggers—prior to impactful automated summarization narratives at scale.

## 5. Pattern interpretation model

The interpretive stance treats outputs as **descriptive ensembles** conditioned on age and context—not prophecy.

### Pattern families (illustrative, non-exhaustive)

- **Multipotentiality bursts:** breadth with sufficiency thresholds across dimensions—paired with scaffolding for narrowing without closure.
- **Deep specialist spark:** standout dimension combinations with narrower breadth—paired with diversification prompts.
- **Builder-operator blend:** coupling practical execution with analytical or systems instincts—paired with environmental matches (hands-on labs, internships).
- **Relational amplifiers:** empathy + collaboration + communication clusters—paired with roles emphasizing facilitation, stewardship, diplomacy.
- **Reflective strategist:** reflective depth + systems + analytical interplay—paired with policy, ethics-adjacent, complex-problem curricula (language remains non-prescriptive).

### Confidence and inconsistency signals

Interpretation distinguishes **confidence bands** stemming from corroborating evidence versus **scatter** implying situational variability, motivational state, misunderstanding, linguistic mismatch, social desirability, or safeguarding concerns.

### Safeguarding escalation

Predefined triggers route to supportive human pathways without diagnostic claims (see Ethics).

## 6. Ethical rules

1. **Do no foreseeable harm.** Default to formulations that widen agency; avoid deterministic language about worth, aptitude ceilings, morality, race, gender, sexuality, neurodiversity diagnoses.
2. **Guardian/educator stewardship.** Respect applicable consent frameworks by jurisdiction; minors’ voices matter but guardians remain accountable partners.
3. **Non-discrimination.** Prohibit discriminatory classifications; audit for disparate impact systematically.
4. **Truthful uncertainty.** Confidence must not be theatrical; unknowns surfaced plainly.
5. **Data minimization.** Collect only what serves stated purposes with explicit retention horizons.
6. **Rightful explainability.** Learners deserve intelligible rationales anchored in their inputs within safety constraints.
7. **Wellbeing first.** Emotional distress narratives trigger supportive escalation paths—not cold analytics.
8. **No manipulation.** Outputs must not nudge covertly toward commercial partners or covert behavioral modification that bypasses autonomy.
9. **Professional humility.** Assessments illuminate development domains; they do not replace psychologists, psychiatrists, or licensed counselors for clinical judgments.

Cross-reference organisational policies in `docs/ethics.md`, `docs/consent-framework.md`, and `docs/student-safety.md` as authoritative supplements.

## 7. AI boundaries

Automated assistants (today or future) operate under strict functional limits:

**Permitted.** Language transformation for clarity tone-matching guidelines; scaffolding question generation drafts under human QA; thematic clustering over anonymized aggregates; personalization of exploratory suggestions bounded by deterministic rules + transparent parameters.

**Prohibited (without narrowly scoped approvals + governance change).**

- Automated clinical diagnoses or prognosis.
- Ranking individuals for punitive resource allocation absent explicit policy audits.
- Opaque personalization that reshapes formative experiences without guardian/educator visibility.
- Generating personally identifying sensitive inferences unrelated to articulated purposes.
- Unsupervised rewriting of formative educational records.

**Operational posture.** Humans retain veto authority on substantive narrative claims; versioning and reproducibility constrain prompt drift.

## 8. Career ecosystem mapping

Interpretations connect developmental patterns plus interests to **ecosystems**, not deterministic job titles early on.

### Layered mapping logic (conceptual)

1. **Cluster domains** linked to evidenced strengths (creative production, analytic modeling, stewardship, facilitation, tinkering iteration, exploratory research, etc.).
2. **Environmental affordances**: studio vs. structured classroom vs. mentorship vs. project studio vs. field immersion.
3. **Progression scaffolding**: exploratory micro-projects → structured programs → bridging credentials—always optional branches.
4. **Labor market stewardship**: highlight growth areas cautiously—dynamic data sources, disclaimers avoiding hype cycles; prioritize skill trajectories transferable across roles.

Maintain explicit separation between fascination with a domain aesthetic (e.g., “space visuals”) versus realistic skill-building anchors.

## 9. Output language guidelines

1. Use **additive** framings (“You show early strength signals in X contexts…”) avoiding deficit-only narratives.
2. Pair strengths with **contextual cultivation levers**, not deterministic outcomes.
3. Employ **confidence hedges** calibrated to evidentiary breadth.
4. Avoid essentialism (“born to be…”); emphasize **plasticity**.
5. When comparing to peers statistically, soften decontextualised rankings; prefer illustrative ranges with caveats.
6. Sensitive dimensions (risk tolerance, social energy, emotional awareness) merit non-judgmental dual descriptions.
7. Offer **reflection prompts**, not commandments.
8. Localize idioms conscientiously once multilingual rollout begins.

Operational cross-reference future detailed tone rules in assessment language collateral already maintained elsewhere (e.g. `docs/assessment-language-rules.md`).

## 10. MVP implementation roadmap

**Phase A – Conceptual crystallization.** Finalize adolescent phase definitions & dimension glossary; circulate with educators for semantic validation.

**Phase B – Minimal signal design.** Pilot item banks emphasizing low-stakes exploratory tasks and reflective journaling prompts (non-scoring formative prototypes permissible prior to calibrated scoring roadmap).

**Phase C – Safeguarding + ethics integration.** Institutional review path; escalate workflow definition; DPIA-aligned data taxonomy.

**Phase D – Transparency assets.** Candidate parent/guardian explainer narratives; abbreviated technical methodology summary for auditors.

**Phase E – Calibrated rollout gating.** Predeclare reliability & fairness checkpoints before transitioning any automated narrative personalization from shadow to surfaced.

Explicitly excludes (for MVP gating completeness): undisclosed personalization, longitudinal predictive claims without validated horizons, integrations promising labor market certainty.

## 11. Open validation questions

Unanswered tensions should be iterated publicly as research—not hidden technical debt:

1. **Construct stability:** Which dimensions meaningfully converge vs. artificially fragment across adolescent development?
2. **Cross-cultural transfer:** Which item stems remain semantically equitable after translation—not merely literal fidelity?
3. **Motivational distortions:** How large are social-desirability and stereotype-threat effects—and which creative item formats blunt them without gaming collapse?
4. **Digital divide artefacts:** Screen vs. conversational vs. tactile modalities—which bias aggregates how?
5. **Guardian divergence:** Where learner self-view and guardian/educator priors collide, whose synthesis ethically leads—and when separately report?
6. **Longitudinal fidelity:** Desired revisit cadence minimizing harm from labelling echoes while benefiting growth tracking privacy budget?
7. **Narrative coherence vs. fidelity trade-off:** Readable stories vs. over-smoothed inaccuracies—measurable tension metrics?
8. **Cluster boundary ethics:** Naming pattern families carries identity risk—how ephemeral should labels remain?
9. **Market mapping lag:** Workforce ontology refresh rates vs. student multi-year arcs—risk communication standards?
10. **AI escalation triggers:** Operational threshold precision vs. false negative/positive trade-offs for safeguarding automation?

Maintain a lightweight living appendix when empirical answers crystallize—without converting unresolved questions into speculative product promises.
