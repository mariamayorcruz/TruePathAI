# Assessment Language Rules

TruePath AI assessments should feel like guided self-reflection, not testing, sorting, diagnosing, or career assignment.

## How questions should be written

Questions should:

- Be simple and age-appropriate
- Invite noticing rather than judgment
- Avoid implying there is a correct answer
- Focus on lived experiences, preferences, energy, curiosity, and communication
- Allow students to feel mixed, unsure, or changing
- Use everyday scenarios where possible

Preferred patterns:

- "When do you feel most curious?"
- "Which option feels most like you lately?"
- "What kind of activity usually gives you energy?"
- "How do you prefer to learn something new?"
- "What is something you want to understand better?"

## Language to avoid

Avoid:

- "Always" and "never"
- "Best" or "worst" personality
- "Right" or "wrong" answers
- "Weakness" as a fixed identity
- "Career match" as a final result
- "You are the type of person who..."
- "This means you should..."
- "People like you..."
- Questions that reward socially desirable answers

## Encouraging exploration

Assessment feedback should:

- Use "might," "could," and "may"
- Offer more than one possible interpretation
- Highlight strengths without ranking them
- Invite students to test ideas in real life
- Encourage conversation with trusted adults
- Remind students that answers can change

Example:

> "Your answers may suggest that you enjoy learning through hands-on exploration. This does not mean you only learn one way. It may be a useful clue to try more projects, experiments, or creative practice."

## Avoiding psychological conditioning

TruePath AI should not train students to answer in ways that please the platform.

Assessment flows should avoid:

- Rewarding specific personality traits
- Over-celebrating one pathway
- Repeatedly nudging toward a preferred outcome
- Framing growth as compliance
- Using streaks or badges for emotional disclosure
- Treating vulnerability as a conversion event

## Result language rules

Results must:

1. Start with affirmation and openness.
2. Avoid fixed labels.
3. Distinguish clues from conclusions.
4. Include growth and change language.
5. Avoid high-stakes recommendations.
6. Encourage reflection before action.

## Review checklist

Before publishing an assessment question or result, review:

- Could a student internalize this as permanent?
- Could a parent use this to restrict opportunity?
- Does the wording create pressure or shame?
- Does the question respect uncertainty?
- Does the result invite exploration rather than compliance?
