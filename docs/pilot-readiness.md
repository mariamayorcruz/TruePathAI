# Pilot Readiness

TruePath AI should enter a school, university, or sponsor-backed pilot only after the product team can clearly explain the student experience, privacy boundaries, consent expectations, and demo limitations.

## Pilot purpose

The first pilot should validate whether TruePath AI helps students feel more curious, understood, and supported without creating labels, scores, or deterministic paths.

The pilot should not validate AI-generated profiles, scoring, production analytics, or automated student recommendations yet.

## Suggested pilot timeline

Use a stage-based timeline rather than a fixed calendar commitment. The pilot should move forward only when the prior stage has clear stakeholder understanding and safety review.

1. Stakeholder review and demo walkthrough
2. Consent, privacy, and school approval planning
3. Facilitated demo sessions with clearly bounded participation
4. Student, parent, counselor, and sponsor feedback review
5. Decision point for whether to build accounts, storage, AI support, or school dashboards

Each stage should have a named owner, documented concerns, and a clear go/no-go decision before the next stage begins.

### Stage 1: Stakeholder review

- Walk school leaders, counselors, parent representatives, and potential sponsors through the demo.
- Review the landing page, onboarding, assessment prototype, student preview, school preview, and pitch page.
- Confirm that the language feels supportive, non-deterministic, and age-appropriate.

### Stage 2: Consent and privacy planning

- Define which students would participate and why.
- Draft parent/guardian consent language.
- Draft student assent language.
- Decide what, if anything, would be collected in a future pilot version.
- Confirm whether school agreements, FERPA review, or district approval are required.

### Stage 3: Facilitated demo sessions

- Use the current product as a guided demo rather than a production tool.
- Make clear that demo previews are static examples.
- Gather feedback from students, parents, educators, counselors, and sponsors.
- Observe whether students understand that the experience opens possibilities and does not define them.

### Stage 4: Safety and usefulness review

- Review student comprehension.
- Review parent and counselor concerns.
- Identify language that could feel too certain, too personal, or too career-directive.
- Decide whether the next version should add accounts, storage, or AI analysis.

## Parent consent considerations

- Parents should understand that TruePath AI is not a diagnostic tool or career assignment system.
- Parents should understand what data is collected before any production pilot begins.
- Parents should know whether student reflections are private, shared, summarized, or aggregated.
- Parents should have a pathway to ask questions, request review, and request deletion where applicable.

## School stakeholder checklist

- School leadership
- Counselors or advisory leads
- Parent/guardian representatives
- Student voice or youth advisors
- Data privacy officer or equivalent reviewer
- Legal/compliance reviewer where needed
- Sponsor or community partner representative, if applicable

## Demo limitations

The current demo:

- Does not create accounts
- Does not store student responses
- Does not run AI analysis
- Does not generate a real student profile
- Does not score or rank students
- Does not create production school analytics
- Does not replace counseling, advising, or parent guidance

## Ethical safeguards

- Exploration before prediction
- Possibilities before labels
- Student agency before automation
- Parent and school trust before data collection
- Human review before AI-generated insight
- Aggregate school value before individual exposure

## Pilot success signals

The pilot should look for evidence that:

- Students feel safe and not judged
- Students understand uncertainty is normal
- Parents see better conversation starters
- Counselors see supportive, ethical use cases
- Schools see value without needing surveillance or ranking
- Sponsors understand the access and impact opportunity
