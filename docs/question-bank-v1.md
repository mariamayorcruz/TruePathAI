# Question Bank v1 (Conceptual)

**Status.** Conceptual design only—no scoring, no AI prompts, no app wiring. Intended for humane assessment design peer review before pilot instrumentation.

**How to read each item.**

- **Target dimensions** indicate what **hypotheses** the response might inform—not labels applied to respondents.
- **May help observe** describes **potential signals** in context with other evidence; single items are weak evidence.
- **Must NOT conclude** states **explicit misuses** reviewers and future models must forbid.

**Language guardrails (global).**

- No predictions of life outcome, innate ceiling, morality, career fate, rank among people, diagnosis, or “what kind of person someone is forever.”
- Items avoid assuming paid lessons, branded devices, tutors, exotic travel, or parent schedules as defaults; roles (parent, caregiver, sibling, neighbor, mentor) stay interchangeable unless the stem requires one for clarity.

**Referenced dimensions (subset for v1 bank).**

`curiosity` · `creativity` · `empathy` · `resilience` · `systems thinking` · `collaboration` · `leadership` · `autonomy` · `communication` · `innovation tendency` · `reflective depth` · `adaptability` · `analytical thinking` · `social energy` · `practical execution`

**Question styles.**

`scenario choice` · `preference choice` · `reflection prompt` · `visual-style choice` · `behavior-based question` · `situational response`

## 1. Discovery Phase (10–12)

**Developmental lens.** Imaginative exploration, concrete-to-emerging abstract reasoning, growing peer awareness, variable stamina for setbacks. Responses often shift week to week—that is expected variability, not contradiction.

---

### Scenario choice — Discovery

**Q-D1**

- **Stem:** Three classmates want to redo a group poster because they think theirs looks “messy.” You already spent a long time on it. Which response feels closest to you?
  - **A.** “Let’s list what feels messy together, then decide if we redo parts or reorganize.”
  - **B.** “Messy isn’t wrong if ideas are strong—could we sharpen the title first?”
  - **C.** “I’d rather redo it quietly on my own if that’s safer.”
- **Formats:** scenario choice  
- **Target dimensions:** collaboration, autonomy, analytical thinking  
- **May help observe:** preference for jointly diagnosing issues vs. asserting a dissenting rationale vs. self-paced repair under social pressure  
- **Must NOT conclude:** “Natural leader vs. follower,” future job fit, innate collaboration talent, morality of any option  

**Q-D2**

- **Stem:** The class pet’s feeding schedule slips for two days—not from neglect—because calendars got confused. Someone blames another student loudly. Which move fits you today?
  - **A.** Suggest resetting the feeding chart visibly for everyone  
  - **B.** Check with the quieter student blamed to hear their side softly  
  - **C.** Step back silently until adults sort it—you dislike conflict bursts  
- **Formats:** scenario choice  
- **Target dimensions:** empathy, systems thinking, resilience  
- **May help observe:** inclination toward restorative structure vs. social repair vs. de-escalation through latency  
- **Must NOT conclude:** courage deficit, antisocial tendencies, predictive discipline risk

## Pilot review checklist (conceptual QA)

Before any implementation:

1. Linguistic leveling pass targeting implicit class/culture-coded references.  
2. Sensory modality diversity plan so non-visual responders aren’t marginalized when visuals ship.  
3. Safeguarding pathway for disclosures of harm routed out-of-band—not algorithmically summarized callously.  
4. Transparency note surfaced to respondent: exploratory items change; fatigue is permissible; skip honored.
